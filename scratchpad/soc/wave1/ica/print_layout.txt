WELCOME TO THE ICA'S 75TH ANNUAL CONFERENCE
Welcome to the 75th Annual Conference of the International Communication Association! We have over
3,500 in-person attendees and an additional 200-plus participants tuning in from remote locations around
the globe (plus eight regional hubs). The conference theme chosen by our president-elect Thomas
Hanitzsch, ICA@75: Disrupting and Consolidating Communication Research, is an invitation to critically reflect
on communication studies as a discipline and ICA as an agent and site of disciplinary development.

The 75th opening plenary session, “ICA@75: Looking Back, Forging Ahead'” (Thursday evening, 12 June,
18:00 – 19:15, Centennial C), starts the conference and will be presented as a hybrid session for our remote
participants as well. This panel features Jefferson Pooley (U of Pennsylvania), Cherian George (Hong Kong
Baptist U), Robin Mansell (London School of Economics & Political Science), and Hanan Badr (U of Salzburg),
in a wide-ranging discussion on the history and future of communication as a discipline, moderated by
Thomas Hanitzsch (LMU Munich).

New members, students, and all those wanting an overview of ICA, the conference, and our place in the field
of communication should be sure to attend the hybrid session, “ICA New Member and Student & Early-Career
Scholar Orientation” (Sat, 14 June, 9:00 – 10:15, Centennial C). This session is designed for all members and
provides an occasion to raise questions regarding how the association works. It will also include a general
overview of ICA as an organization, an overview of the conference, and ways to participate further in ICA. This
is your opportunity to interact with the Executive Committee of ICA and help shape the association and its
future direction! You can also catch the Executive Committee members one on one at the annual ice cream
social (Sun, 15 June, 13:00 – 13:30, Exhibit Hall, Centennial D).

Another annual event to look forward to are the three ICA-wide Interactive Paper/Poster Sessions. All three
sessions will be from 13:30 to 14:45 on Fri, June 12, Sat, June 13, and Sun, June 14, in Centennial Foyer. They
will feature over 30 individual papers each day, encompassing all of ICA’s divisions and interest groups.
Each day will highlight a best poster winner and runner-up whose posters will stay up for the entirety of the
conference.

The (HYBRID) 75th Annual ICA Presidential Address, Fellows' Induction, Awards Ceremony, and Member
Business Meeting (Sat, 14 June, 16:30 – 17:45, Centennial E) features annual awards presentations, induction
of new ICA Fellows, and the presidential address from Silvio Waisbord (George Washington U).

In addition to the breadth and depth of scholarship, we have a fantastic 75th Anniversary Timeline, a fun “ICA
History Museum” full of official artifacts (and some fun tchotchkes!), and a special mini-documentary that will
make its debut at the opening plenary.

Closing out the conference, join us at the 75th Anniversary Closing Toast (Mon, 16 June, 16:30 – 17:30,
Centennial Foyer.)

As always, we have made every effort to make the conference accessible to remote and onsite attendees
with various accommodations made for hearing, vision, and medical conditions, as well as maximizing
inclusion and belonging of LGBTQIA+ scholars, Black and indigenous scholars, neurodivergent scholars, and
others. Please check the Conference Inclusion and Accessibility section of this program for more information
on the time and location of AA meetings, the quiet and religious observance room, the nursing parents' room,
childcare and the family play zone, gender-neutral restrooms, and more.

Lastly, we want to thank all of you who worked to overcome numerous funding, logistical, and political
obstacles to make it to our 75th Anniversary conference, who persevered with your plans to attend in
the United States despite numerous opportunities to bow out and multiple factors that may have given
you pause. We also thank those of you who couldn’t make that work, but who were willing to follow the
new pathways we created to ensure you could participate online. We value ALL of your voices and your
perspectives, and it is our sincere hope that the diverse programming at this conference will generate
intellectual growth, thought-provoking engagement, and a memorable experience for everyone involved,
and that each of you will come away from this conference energized, full of new ideas, and with positive
memories of your ICA community. Whether you are joining us in person in Denver, online as an individual
(from the comfort of your home), or from a regional hub, we can’t wait to see you!

Laura Sawyer
Executive Director
ICA Headquarters, Washington DC
WELCOME FROM THE CONFERENCE PLANNER
Welcome to Denver! Welcome to the 75th conference of the International Communication Association!

This year's ICA conference theme is ICA@75: Disrupting and Consolidating Communication Research. The
theme was intended to provide multiple opportunities to critically reflect on our field of scholarship and
the role of ICA in shaping it. The sessions addressing the theme honor the institutions, thinkers, and ideas
that contributed to the foundation of the discipline and engage in a thoughtful examination of the field’s
development, including the paths it has taken and those it has overlooked.

I am delighted that the theme call attracted such an impressive number of submissions. Eleven theme panels
made it into the conference program, where you will find them under "ICA@25 Theme Sessions." The titles
of many of these panels explicitly pick up on the theme's framing, leveraging on the productive dialectics of
“disruption” and “consolidation.” Furthermore, the theme has sparked an impressive range of pre- and post-
conferences and conference papers submitted to the 33 ICA divisions and interest groups.

Judging from the titles and descriptions in the conference program, conversations in these sessions
gravitate around the history of our field and ICA, the future of academic publishing and our meritocratic
system, the emotional toll brought on by the high productivity demands, persistent social and global
inequalities in scholarship, the new challenges posed by generative AI to the scholarly world, and other
issues central to disciplinary development.

When we, in early 2024, decided to put the notion of "disruption" in the conference theme, we could not have
anticipated what eventually happened in the United States one year later. We have witnessed a massive
attack on academic freedom, the integrity of science, and higher education in the country. It is worth noting,
however, that higher education is under attack in many places in the world, as the 2025 update to the global
Academic Freedom Index demonstrates.

In response to these developments, we created additional space at ICA25 for our members to engage in
conversations about threats to academic freedom, the potential implications for ICA, and possible responses
by the scholarly community. Let me point your attention to three sessions that will address these more recent
developments:

The first of these panels, Science Under Fire, moderated by Jessica Piotrowski (U of Amsterdam), will
follow up on a similar panel held at ICA24. The session broadens the conversation to address the growing
challenges communication scholars face as they navigate a climate of heightened scrutiny and hostility.
The panel brings together experts from across the globe and various domains of communication studies,
including Dhavan Shah (U of Wisconsin - Madison), Hanan Badr (U of Salzburg), Saskia Witteborn (The
Chinese U of Hong Kong), Jiyoung Han (KAIST), and Amanda Third (Western Sydney U). With concrete
(and sometimes harrowing) stories, the discussion aims to shed light on the lived experiences of affected
researchers, fostering a deeper understanding of the systemic issues at play. Ultimately, the panel aims to
identify actionable solutions and explore what ICA can do to support scholars and uphold the integrity of
communication research in an increasingly contentious environment.

The second panel, U.S. Academy Under Attack: Challenges, Implications, and Ways Forward, will explore
the significant changes and challenges facing education in the U.S. since January 2025. The session
focuses on universities across the country suffering from systematic defunding, ideological pressure, and
state interference. Panelists discuss the administration's restrictive immigration policy, which has affected
international students, some of whom ended up in detention for expressing political views differing from
those of the administration. The panel will delve into these pressing issues, discuss their implications, and
consider potential pathways forward for education in this evolving landscape. Put together by Kate Kenski
(U of Arizona) and moderated by Daniel Kreiss (U of North Carolina), panelists include Heesoo Jang (U of
Massachusetts-Amherst), Regina Lawrence (U of Oregon), Josephine Lukito (U of Texas, Austin), Rebekah
Tromble (George Washington U), Josh Pasek (U of Michigan), Sarah Shugars (Rutgers U), and Mike Wagner (U
of Wisconsin-Madison).

The third panel, U.S. Policy Climate and Far-Right Pressure Tactics: The Impact on International Scholars
and Strategies of Resistance, is a hybrid session based on a collaboration between the ICA Oceania Hub in
Aotearoa, New Zealand, and the main ICA conference. The panel addresses the ongoing attacks on higher
                                                                                                               11
education in the U.S., focusing on the immediate consequences and long-term implications for international
communication scholarship, the academic freedom of communication scholars, and ICA as a key player in the

RELIGIOUS & CULTURAL ACCOMMODATIONS
Religious Observance/Multi-Faith Room
We know that travel can make adhering to religious observance tricky. We are providing a religious
observance room (Mt. Wilson Room, 3rd Floor at the top of the escalators/across from the restrooms at the
Grand Hyatt), which can be used for quiet religious observance or quiet contemplation for any faith. Any items
needed for your personal use in prayer (such as malas, prayer mats, cushions, etc.) should be taken with
you when you leave the room, as the room is not monitored and we cannot guarantee your items will not be
misplaced. If you remove your shoes, please line them up along the wall at the doorway. Quiet meditation and
yoga are also allowed in this room.

Religious Schedule Conflicts
Those with religious observations that affect their availability for certain conference sessions are asked to
enter that information in the “Notes to Admin” comment box in their initial paper or session submission (this
information is not viewable by those who are reviewing your work, only by planners and staff). You can go in
and change this even after being accepted, as well, but it must be done prior to the first week of February, as
we then close the system to schedule the program. If you are Orthodox and observe the Sabbath, for example,
you may note this in your paper/session and ask that we not schedule your presentations between sundown
Friday and sundown Saturday. We can, and are happy to, accommodate these requests 100% of the time as
long as we have advanced notice. If you are reading this information after the deadline to make the request,
you may contact Laura Sawyer to see if there is anything that can be done. We will work with you as much
as possible to resolve the issue, with the caveat that once the schedule goes live, presenters book flights

Special Note Regarding Religious Holidays/Observances
The ICA conference typically takes place on the U.S. Memorial Day weekend - globally, this weekend has
historically been a good weekend to get affordable prices locked in for our conference attendees. That said,
this timing also sometimes overlaps with a religious holiday for some of our 4,000+ attendees from 80+
different countries. In Washington DC in 2019 the ICA conference overlapped with an important religious
holiday, a situation which could not be corrected once it was discovered because the contract had been
signed in 2014 and there was no ability to shift the dates. We make every effort to consider (and avoid
treading upon) all global holidays as blackout dates for the conference in all future contracting, with the
knowledge that we are nonetheless bound by the expectation among the ICA community that the conference
will occur from late May to late June.

TRAVEL SAFETY
Travel Safety
As with any destination, it is important to:
• Remove your badge when outside the venue/your hotel (this is the #1 mistake we see attendees making
    out and about!) Be aware of your surroundings, especially if a stranger suddenly engages you in a lively
    conversation (often a distraction technique for pickpockets)
• Do not blatantly stare at maps/your phone’s GPS while walking, which sends a clear message that you are
    a tourist.
• Take care when crossing the street, especially if traffic goes in a different direction than what you are
    used to at home.
• Be discreet about the cash you’re carrying, and use your hotel safe to store your passport rather than
    carrying it with you if possible.
• As with any downtown area, safety can vary by neighborhood. If you do decide to stay outside the ICA
    housing block, please be careful when booking your lodging and cross reference your location with
    several sources to ensure you are in a safe area.

Harassment
ICA takes its attendees’ and members’ safety seriously. By registering for the conference, every attendee,
exhibitor, sponsor, and speaker has agreed to abide by the Conference Code of Conduct. You have the right
to attend the conference without being the subject of harassment based on your ethnicity, gender, sexual
orientation, or any other issue. If you are being harassed, notice that someone else is being harassed, or have
other concerns about inappropriate behavior, please contact a member of conference staff immediately.
Conference staff can most easily be found at the registration desk, and will be wearing ribbons that say
STAFF (please take care to find actual ICA staff and not student volunteers who may also be working in this
area – volunteers are typically wearing round buttons that say “I’m a volunteer!”). You may also email both
Julie Arnold, Director of Governance & Member Services, and Laura Sawyer, Executive Director, directly with
any concerns. Information shared will be kept confidential. We will be happy to help participants contact
hotel/venue security or local law enforcement, provide escorts, or otherwise assist those experiencing
harassment to feel safe for the duration of the conference, and to take action to remove those acting
inappropriately. We reserve the right to revoke access to the conference for anyone harassing another
individual. We keep a record of all incidents and reserve the right to ban individuals from future conferences.

SHOULD A PROBLEM ARISE WHILE YOU ARE ONSITE AT THE ICA MEETING
If you have any problem or negative experience related to accessibility—including issues with housing,
sessions, harassment, or any other accessibility-related issue that takes place within the conference venue
or blocked hotel--please email Laura Sawyer (lsawyer@icahdq.org), ICA Executive Director and/or Julie Arnold
(jarnold@icahdq.org), Director of Governance and Member Services. In addition to meeting these needs, we
keep a record of all requests and our ability to meet them to improve each year; we also provide feedback on
accessibility issues to our hotel partners and the cities in which we meet. Please note that a reply may not
be immediate given the constraints and demands of our entire staff being onsite for the conference and in
meetings for part of each day.

                            General Call for Papers
  The Journal of Gulf Studies invites submissions that promote interdisciplinary
  approaches within the context of area studies, with a specific focus on the Gulf region.
  We welcome academic articles, book reviews, and conference or symposium reports
  that align with the journal’s mission and thematic areas.



  Submission Guidelines
  • Academic articles: 6,000–8,000 words
  • Book reviews: 1,200–1,500 words
  • Conference/symposium reports: 1,500–2,000 words
  • All submissions must follow the Intellect Harvard citation style
  To submit your work or learn more about our publication process, please visit our
  Journal Editors and Contributors page (via the QR code), which includes information on
  publishing agreements, copyright, accessibility, and more.

                                                    scholarly conversation on the Gulf and
                                                    its global intersections.
